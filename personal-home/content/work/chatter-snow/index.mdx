casestudy:
  problem: >
    There was no infrastructure. A shared Gmail account and an Instagram profile were the
    entire operational stack — no domain, no organizational identity, no records, no system to
    track donated gear, coordinate volunteers, manage events, handle finances, or organize
    donations. Everything lived in DMs, threads, and individual people's heads, with no
    visibility for community members. Core issue: Chatter couldn't tell people "here's what we
    have available," and — more importantly for a group organizing toward nonprofit status —
    it couldn't tell anyone, including itself, what kind of impact it was actually having.
  context: >
    Chatter Snow is not a nonprofit yet; the group is actively organizing to become one. That
    framing drove the whole project: the infrastructure exists so the organization can measure
    and evidence its impact — gear distributed, members served, events run, money in and out —
    which is exactly what a nonprofit application and future funders will ask for. Add a
    volunteer team, effectively no budget, a growing inventory of donated ski and snowboard
    gear, and an active community that wanted to know what was available. Being both a board
    member and the technical lead meant seeing the operational need and the technical
    constraints firsthand.
  goals:
    - Stand up real infrastructure — domain, email, identity, accounts — where none existed
    - Create a centralized inventory system
    - Enable the community to see available gear without a DM or an email
    - Capture the operational data needed to evidence impact for nonprofit formation
    - Reduce manual admin work
    - Build a foundation for future growth (volunteers, events, giving, etc.)
    - Keep it maintainable by volunteers — not over-engineered
  constraints:
    - Effectively no budget — a pre-nonprofit group with no funding stream
    - Starting from zero infrastructure, not migrating an existing system
    - Volunteer-driven — the system couldn't be complex
    - Needed to launch quickly; the community was waiting
    - No dedicated devops/infrastructure team
    - Growing organization that needed the system to scale
  research: >
    As board member and director, the operational workflows, community expectations, budget
    reality, and volunteer capabilities were already well understood. Key insight: with nothing
    to migrate, the risk wasn't legacy — it was over-building. Buy the commodity pieces (domain,
    mail, hosting), build only what's specific to Chatter, start narrow with inventory, and
    design for expansion.
  architecture: >
    Core philosophy: minimal, maintainable, bought where possible. Two surfaces ship from one
    codebase — the public site at chattersnow.org and the operations portal at
    portal.chattersnow.org. Next.js + TypeScript power both, talking to Vercel Edge Functions
    that handle mutations, auth, and logic, backed by Supabase/PostgreSQL for inventory,
    events, volunteers, and financials, with Zoho for organizational email and Cloudflare for
    CDN and DNS. Next.js + TypeScript enabled fast iteration and full-stack capability with
    familiar tools; Supabase + PostgreSQL suited simple relational data at low cost; Zoho gave
    the group a real organizational mailbox to replace the shared Gmail account; Vercel kept
    deployment and scaling simple; Cloudflare provided CDN, DNS, and DDoS protection.
    Deliberately not built: a custom CMS (Supabase's admin interface was good enough),
    extensive reporting (kept focus on core operations), a mobile app (responsive web was
    sufficient), and complex authentication (simple role-based access was enough).
  design: >
    Functional and clear, focused on usability over visual polish — volunteers needed to
    understand the system quickly. The public site tells the community who Chatter is and what
    gear is available. The ops portal is where the organization actually runs: inventory
    management, volunteer coordination and shift signup, event creation, and financial tracking
    for donations and expenses. Portal access is invite-only and role-based — it holds real
    member and financial data, so there is no public entry point into it.
  implementation: >
    Phase zero was infrastructure that didn't exist: registering chattersnow.org, moving off the
    shared Gmail account to organizational email, and setting up DNS, hosting, and accounts. The
    initial build covered inventory management (the core problem), the community-facing gear
    view, and basic volunteer coordination. The platform then evolved into the ops portal —
    event management, donation and financial tracking, and member communication. Rough
    timeline: about two weeks of discovery and planning, three to four weeks for the MVP build,
    two weeks of testing and iteration, and one week to launch — built with volunteer time
    alongside the technical lead role.
  challenges:
    - Starting from zero — no domain, no org email, no records to migrate or learn from
    - Requirement creep — once people saw the system working, everyone wanted new features
    - Designing for volunteers — complex features had to stay simple to use
    - Data accuracy — reconstructing inventory history that only existed in DMs and memory
    - Scaling — Vercel serverless can handle growth, but database optimization matters as data grows
    - Feature priority — deciding what's MVP versus what can wait
  decisions:
    - title: "Use Supabase, not Firebase"
      description: "Chose Supabase over Firebase for the database layer."
      tradeoffs: >
        Better suited to complex relational data (gear, volunteers, events, and financials are
        all interconnected), at the cost of slightly more infrastructure to manage than
        Firebase.
    - title: "Buy the commodity, build the difference"
      description: "Introduced Zoho as the org's email rather than building or self-administering anything, so the custom work stayed on what's specific to Chatter."
      tradeoffs: >
        Replacing the shared Gmail account with a real organizational mailbox took a day
        instead of a project, at the cost of a small recurring bill on a near-zero budget.
    - title: "Public site and ops portal are separate surfaces"
      description: "Split the community-facing site (chattersnow.org) from the operations portal (portal.chattersnow.org)."
      tradeoffs: >
        Simple, focused experience for members and an easier boundary to secure and scale, at
        the cost of some code duplication.
    - title: "Keep the portal invite-only for now"
      description: "Left the ops portal fully gated rather than shipping a public demo login alongside it."
      tradeoffs: >
        No risk of exposing real member or financial data, at the cost of not being able to
        show the system to a prospective volunteer, board candidate, or funder without
        provisioning them an account — a public demo needs a separate seeded dataset, which is
        deferred work rather than solved work.
    - title: "Start with inventory, design for expansion"
      description: "Scoped the initial build to inventory management only, but architected it to support more."
      tradeoffs: >
        Enabled a quick, focused launch, and the architecture now supports adding volunteers,
        events, and financials without a redesign — at the cost of more upfront design and
        architecture work.
    - title: "Vercel serverless functions, not a traditional backend"
      description: "Used Vercel Edge/serverless functions for all API logic instead of a standalone backend service."
      tradeoffs: >
        Simple scaling, low cost, and minimal devops, at the cost of cold-start latency —
        acceptable for a nonprofit's usage patterns.
  result: >
    A group that ran on a shared Gmail account and an Instagram profile now has a domain,
    organizational email, a public site, and an operations portal at portal.chattersnow.org
    covering inventory, volunteers, events, donations, and financials. The community can see available gear without
    sending a DM, and for the first time the organization is generating the operational record
    it needs to show its impact as it organizes toward nonprofit status. Specific figures are
    still TBD, pending more usage data.
  lessonLearned:
    - "Infrastructure is the measurement — Chatter can't evidence its impact for nonprofit formation without a system that records what it does"
    - "Start narrow, design wide — focusing on inventory first made launch possible, but the architecture allowed for expansion"
    - "Buy the commodity — a paid mailbox and managed hosting cost less than the volunteer hours self-administering them would have"
    - "Design for your actual users — volunteers have limited patience for complexity, so every feature needs to be justified"
    - "Access boundaries are a product decision — a system holding real member and financial data can't be casually opened up, and showing it to outsiders is its own piece of work"
    - "Get hands dirty — being on the board meant understanding real problems firsthand, an edge an outside consultant wouldn't have"

## See it running

- **[chattersnow.org](https://chattersnow.org)** — the public site: who Chatter is, and what
  gear is available to the community.
- **[portal.chattersnow.org](https://portal.chattersnow.org)** — the operations portal, where
  inventory, volunteers, events, and finances are actually managed. It holds real member and
  financial data, so access is invite-only and role-based; there's no public login. A seeded
  demo instance is planned but not live yet. For a system you can walk through end to end, see
  the [Personal Finance OS](/projects/personal-finance-os) case study, which has a public demo
  account.

## Metrics

Technical metrics (deployment time, uptime, page load time, hosting cost) and organizational
impact metrics (time saved, member engagement, admin efficiency gains) are still TBD — they'll
be added here once more usage data is available. Producing those impact numbers is a large part
of why the platform exists: Chatter is organizing toward nonprofit status, and the operational
record the portal generates is what that case will be built on.

## What's next

Near-term: optimize based on feedback from live usage, and stand up a seeded demo instance of
the portal so the system can be shown to prospective volunteers, board candidates, and funders
without provisioning real accounts. Medium-term: reporting and analytics aimed squarely at the
nonprofit application — gear distributed, members served, events run, money in and out. Long-term: potentially a mobile app and fundraising integrations.
